# Skill: Debias Agent

## Description

This skill provides a structured framework for an AI agent to detect, highlight, and mitigate various cognitive, statistical, 
and analytical biases present in both qualitative and quantitative analyses generated by AI or human authors.

You can critique human or AI biases, you can also be self critical. 

Try to use a scientific mindset. 

## Targeted Biases & Detection Criteria

### 1. Simpson's Paradox

* **Definition:** A phenomenon in probability and statistics in which a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when these groups are combined.

* **Detection Trigger:** Claims made about aggregated data without examining underlying subgroup trends or demographic breakdowns.

* **Mitigation Step:** Prompt the analysis to disaggregate data by relevant categories (e.g., demographics, time periods, cohorts) to verify if the overall trend holds across subgroups.

### 2. $p$-Hacking (Data Dredging)

* **Definition:** The misuse of data analysis to find patterns in data that can be presented as statistically significant, when in fact there is no real underlying effect.

* **Detection Trigger:** Multiple testing without adjusting threshold significance levels, selective reporting of significant results, or changing variables/subgroups post-hoc.

* **Mitigation Step:** Require pre-specification of hypotheses, report all tested variables/metrics, apply corrections for multiple comparisons (e.g., Bonferroni correction), and highlight non-significant findings.

### 3. Confirmation Bias

* **Definition:** The tendency to search for, interpret, favor, and recall information in a way that confirms or supports one's prior beliefs or values.

* **Detection Trigger:** One-sided arguments, cherry-picked data points, ignoring counter-evidence, or framing ambiguous evidence solely in support of the primary hypothesis.

* **Mitigation Step:** Actively seek out counter-arguments, introduce a "red team" perspective, and evaluate alternative explanations with equal weight.

### 4. Anchoring Bias

* **Definition:** A cognitive bias where an individual relies too heavily on an initial piece of information offered (the "anchor") when making decisions or estimates.

* **Detection Trigger:** Subsequent estimates, forecasts, or valuations remaining overly close to an initial baseline or historical metric without sufficient justification.

* **Mitigation Step:** Re-evaluate calculations from first principles using independent data sources, ignoring initial reference points during preliminary assessment.

### 5. Regression to the Mean

* **Definition:** The statistical phenomenon where an extreme variable measurement will tend to be closer to the average on its subsequent measurement.

* **Detection Trigger:** Attributing natural variations or statistical fluctuations in extreme performance (high or low) directly to a specific intervention or treatment.

* **Mitigation Step:** Check for baseline measurement frequency, establish control groups, and track longitudinal trends over extended periods.

### 6. Omitted Confounder Bias (Omitted Variable Bias)

* **Definition:** Occurs when a model or analysis incorrectly omits one or more important causal factors, leading to biased estimates of the remaining variables.

* **Detection Trigger:** Asserting direct causation between two variables ($X \rightarrow Y$) without accounting for shared external variables ($Z$).

* **Mitigation Step:** Map out causal diagrams (DAGs - Directed Acyclic Graphs), identify potential unmeasured confounders, and evaluate environmental or context-level factors.

### 7. Selection Bias

* **Definition:** The bias introduced by the selection of individuals, groups, or data for analysis such that proper randomization is not achieved, ensuring that the sample obtained is not representative of the intended population.

* **Detection Trigger:** Generalizing findings from non-random samples, voluntary response surveys, survivorship sets, or attrition-heavy longitudinal studies.

* **Mitigation Step:** Assess sampling methodology, report non-response or drop-out rates, adjust weights to match target demographics, and limit conclusions strictly to the sampled population.

### 8. Survivorship Bias

* **Definition:** A form of selection bias where the analyst focuses only on successful cases or surviving entities while ignoring failures that did not pass a selection process.

* **Detection Trigger:** Drawing conclusions about strategies, traits, or risk factors solely by studying successful subjects (e.g., successful companies, surviving patients, top performers) without evaluating failed peers.

* **Mitigation Step:** Actively incorporate data from non-survivors or historical dropouts to assess true baseline success rates and avoid overestimating success probabilities.

### 9. Base Rate Fallacy (Neglect of Prior Probability)

* **Definition:** The tendency to ignore general contextual probability information (base rate) in favor of specific, individualized, or descriptive information.

* **Detection Trigger:** Over-estimating conditional probabilities or rare event likelihoods based solely on test sensitivity or descriptive similarity without factoring in overall population prevalence.

* **Mitigation Step:** Apply Bayes' Theorem explicitly to incorporate baseline population rates before determining updated posterior probabilities.

## Workflow Implementation

```
[ Input Analysis (Qualitative / Quantitative) ]
                       │
                       ▼
         [ Phase 1: Bias Identification ]
  • Scan text for statistical anomalies and logical fallacies
  • Cross-reference against target bias inventory
                       │
                       ▼
         [ Phase 2: Impact Assessment ]
  • Determine severity (High / Medium / Low)
  • Identify affected conclusions or metrics
                       │
                       ▼
         [ Phase 3: Debiasing & Refinement ]
  • Rewrite assertions with nuanced framing
  • Request missing data or disaggregated metrics
  • Propose alternative hypotheses
                       │
                       ▼
           [ Output: Debiased Analysis ]

```
